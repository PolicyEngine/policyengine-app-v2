Gifts you make to charity by December 31 count toward your 2026 taxes, the first year of three new federal rules that change what a gift saves. [GiveCalc](https://givecalc.org), our free calculator, applies all three, along with state and local income taxes, and shows what a gift costs you after tax.

Congress enacted the rules in sections 70111, 70424 and 70425 of the [One Big Beautiful Bill Act](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm), which President Trump signed on July 4, 2025. Each household below lives in Texas, which has no state income tax, so its gift lowers only its federal income tax.

## Filers who take the standard deduction

From 2026, filers who don't itemize can deduct up to $1,000 of cash gifts, or $2,000 on a joint return, on top of the standard deduction. The single filer's $1,000 gift lowers their tax by $220 at their 22% rate, and a $2,000 gift saves them the same $220. In 2025, a gift lowered federal income tax only for filers who itemized, so the $1,000 gift saved them nothing.

Filers can count cash they give directly to a charity toward this deduction, but not gifts into a donor-advised fund or to a supporting organization.

## Filers who itemize

Itemizers now deduct only the part of their gifts above 0.5% of their adjusted gross income. The $200,000 earner, who has $20,000 of mortgage interest and $8,000 of property tax, deducts nothing for the first $1,000 they give, 0.5% of their income. On a $10,000 gift, they deduct $9,000 and save $2,160 at their 24% rate; in 2025, they could deduct all $10,000.

## Filers in the top bracket

In 2026, the 37% bracket starts at $640,600 of taxable income for single filers and $768,700 for joint filers. Filers whose income before itemized deductions reaches it give up 2/37 of their itemized deductions, gifts included, up to 2/37 of their income in the bracket. Once their income in the bracket exceeds their itemized deductions, each extra dollar they deduct saves 35 cents in federal income tax, against 37 cents in 2025. For the $1 million earner, an extra $10,000 gift lowers taxable income by $9,459, 35/37 of $10,000, and saves $3,500 at the 37% rate.

## Giving two years at once

After each US result, GiveCalc checks whether giving two years' gifts in one year would save more. For example, a [single filer in Texas earning $120,000](https://givecalc.org/?amount=10000&income=120000&state=TX&year=2026&property_tax=6000) pays $6,000 in property tax. If they give $10,000 in each of 2026 and 2027, they take the standard deduction both years and save $220 a year. If they give $20,000 in 2026 and nothing in 2027, they save $1,874 more over the two years. In 2026 they itemize, deducting the gift above the floor along with their property and sales taxes.

## What GiveCalc counts

GiveCalc runs PolicyEngine's open-source model of your household twice, with and without the gift, and reports how much the gift lowers your federal, state and local taxes. Enter your own state to see its income tax as well. GiveCalc models cash gifts to public charities; it leaves out gifts of shares or property, and state tax credits that depend on which charity you choose. We ran these examples on November 27, 2026, with version 2.49.2 of PolicyEngine's US model.

[Try GiveCalc](https://givecalc.org) with your own household, or read its [code](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/givecalc).
