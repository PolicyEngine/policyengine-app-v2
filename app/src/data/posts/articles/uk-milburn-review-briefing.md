On 17 November 2026 the Department for Work and Pensions [published](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/from-a-welfare-state-to-a-working-state-a-new-settlement-for-the-next-generation) _From a Welfare State to a Working State_. It is Alan Milburn's independent review of the rise in the number of young people not in education, employment or training (NEET), which it puts at nearly one million people aged 16 to 24, one in eight (paragraph 6).
- **Universal Credit (UC).** For claimants under 25, the review proposes a Youth Allowance paid at today's under-25 standard allowance, with a higher rate for those living independently. It also proposes an end to the UC health element and the Work Capability Assessment for most claimants in that age group (paragraphs 744 to 748).
- **Personal Independence Payment (PIP).** It proposes replacing PIP for 18 to 24-year-olds with three new forms of support.
- **Other proposals.** These include a statutory guarantee of a place in education, training or work for 18 to 24-year-olds, and payments to employers who hire young people. "When fiscal conditions allow", it proposes lower employer National Insurance on the earnings of employees aged 21 to 24 in retail and hospitality.

This briefing covers the proposals we model: what they do, our estimates of their effect on the public finances and household incomes, how those compare with the review's own estimates, and which proposals we don't model.

## What the review proposes

The review's changes to benefits apply to young people as they enter the benefit system, and to young people already claiming:

- **New claims** move to the new system once each area has a Youth Opportunity Service, which the review expects in every area by 2029 (paragraph 769).
- **Existing claimants** move at their scheduled reassessment, after six months' preparation, with the move completed within at most five years (paragraphs 770 and 773). Their current payments continue until the replacement support is in place. The review doesn't say whether payment levels are protected after the move.
- **The savings** start in 2029-30 (paragraph 742).

The review makes other proposals that reach young people, which our dashboard does not model:

- **Opportunity and Support Guarantees.** A statutory entitlement for every 18 to 24-year-old to a place in education, training or work, and to employment, health and wider support, delivered by a Youth Opportunity Service in each area of England. The review says these must be in place before the benefit changes (paragraphs 675 to 705).
- **Youth Jobs Grant.** An employer that hires a 16 to 24-year-old who has been NEET for six months, whether or not the young person claims benefits, receives £3,000. It receives £2,000 more for a young person who needs "significant additional support". It receives another £2,000 if it has fewer than 50 employees. The review expects about 60,000 grants a year (paragraphs 275 to 277).
- **Apprenticeships.** The employer incentive rises from £2,000 to £2,500 and extends to employers who pay the Levy, and training providers receive a new £2,000 incentive (paragraph 280).
- **Jobs Guarantee.** A young person who is NEET after 12 months, instead of 18 today, is offered a paid job for six months (paragraphs 279 and 680 to 681).
- **Care Leaver Transition Allowance.** Up to £50 a week for six months for care leavers who are NEET or "at serious risk" of becoming NEET, conditional on agreed education, training or work activity (paragraph 71).
- **A duty to engage.** Each young person agrees a Participation Plan. A young person who "persistently refuses to engage" would ultimately have the Youth Allowance stopped, and the review says sanctions "should be rare" (paragraphs 106 and 712 to 719).
- **The replacements for PIP.** The review sets the principles for the Health and Disability Fund, the Additional Cost Allowance and the Severe and Complex Needs Insurance, but not their rates or eligibility rules (paragraph 751).
- **Disability Living Allowance for children** runs to age 18 instead of 16 (paragraph 760).
- **Civic service.** A 13-week programme, with extra financial support of an amount the review doesn't give for those taking part for 8 to 24 hours a week (paragraphs 682 to 686).
- **Self-employment.** A £3,000 start-up grant, disregarded when the Youth Allowance is calculated (box after paragraph 681).
- **Further education.** The review proposes that funding for 16 to 19-year-olds rises automatically with participation, and that new routes into further education open for 18 to 24-year-olds (paragraphs 445 to 459). It estimates over 150,000 more 18 to 24-year-olds in further education each year in England by 2036-37 (paragraph 450). Its education line is £2.1 billion in 2036-37 (Table 2).

It proposes no change to the minimum wage (paragraphs 285 and 286).

## Proposal by proposal

**UC health element.** [Current law: the amount of the health element in 2029-30, for awards from before and after April 2026. Its full name is the limited capability for work and work-related activity (LCWRA) element.] The review proposes ending it, and the Work Capability Assessment, for most claimants under 25 (paragraphs 744 and 745). We remove it for all of them.
- **How we model it.** An award no longer includes the element where the claimant with limited capability for work and work-related activity is under 25. It keeps the element where a joint claimant aged 25 or over has that capability.
- **What we keep.** We leave the assessment status in place, so the work allowance applies as it does today.
- **The benefit cap.** An award without the element is no longer exempt from the benefit cap on that ground. Where the cap applies, net income falls by more than the element's value.
- **The saving.** We estimate £[F3] billion in 2029-30, with employment held fixed.
- **What we don't model.** The review proposes that during serious illness, injury or intensive treatment young people "should be able to pause support", with financial help from the local fund where longer-term needs are not yet known (paragraph 746). It also proposes that the duty to engage is suspended (paragraphs 712 to 719). We model neither.

**Youth Allowance and the work allowance.** The review proposes that the Youth Allowance pays what the UC standard allowance pays today, except for young people living independently.
- **Who gets the higher rate.** [F-share]% of awards to claimants under 25 include a housing costs element. The review's annex models the higher rate for those living independently as the standard allowance for claimants aged 25 and over, and uses receipt of the UC housing element as the marker for living independently. We do the same.
- **The higher rate.** In 2026-27 terms it would be £86.32 a month more for a single claimant (£424.90 against £338.58). [Give 2029-30 amounts from the model.]
- **The work allowance.** The review gives no amount. We use [our assumption]. A young person working [n] hours a week at the minimum wage would keep £[F18] a month more of their earnings than today.
- **What we leave as it is.** The review doesn't say how the Youth Allowance treats the housing, child, carer and childcare elements, the taper or the benefit cap, so we leave all of them as they are today.
- **The cost.** Together these two changes cost £[F5] billion in 2029-30.

**PIP.** The review proposes replacing PIP for 18 to 24-year-olds with three forms of support:
- a local, discretionary Health and Disability Fund, for needs that are "immediate, one-off or may change" (paragraph 752);
- an Additional Cost Allowance, not means-tested, for disability-related extra costs that are "significant, well established and ongoing" (paragraph 754);
- a Severe and Complex Needs Insurance, for "a small minority", paying "above the maximum benefit rates available today" (paragraph 756).

It sets no rates or eligibility rules for any of them. Its annex calls its own split of claimants between them "illustrative assumptions". The review says that for many young people the replacement "would pay less cash, at least to begin with" (paragraph 737).
- **How we model it.** We end PIP for 18 to 24-year-olds and model no replacement awards. With employment held fixed, our estimate is the upper end of the saving and of the fall in incomes.
- **What follows PIP.** PIP also qualifies people for other support:
  - Carer's Allowance and the carer element, for the people who care for them;
  - disability additions in means-tested benefits;
  - exemption from the benefit cap.

  The review doesn't say what happens to these, so we keep today's rules, and they end with the PIP award. This adds £[F4b] billion to the saving. If the replacements carried these entitlements, the saving and the fall in incomes would each be smaller by up to that amount.
- **The saving.** £[F4] billion in 2029-30: £[F4a] billion of PIP itself and £[F4b] billion from the entitlements that follow it.
- **Scotland.** [Scottish recipients of Adult Disability Payment, which the data record as PIP, are included / left out.] The review's costing covers PIP and UC across the UK, while Adult Disability Payment is the Scottish Government's. [Say what this choice does to the comparison with the review's UK-wide costing.]

**Employer National Insurance.** The review proposes "a 5-year glide path to lower National Insurance costs for workers aged 21 to 24" (paragraph 269).
- **Where it starts.** It starts with retail and hospitality, which the review says provide 37% of the jobs held by 16 to 24-year-olds, with other sectors to follow (paragraphs 50 and 270).
- **What changes.** In those sectors, employers would pay no employer National Insurance on the first £10,000 a year of these employees' earnings, up from £5,000. The threshold would be reached over five years, at a pace "set at each fiscal event" (paragraphs 269 to 271).
- **The rationale.** At £10,000, the review notes, an employer could employ a 21-year-old for 15 hours a week at the minimum wage without paying employer National Insurance.
- **What's not given, and what stays.** It gives no start date and no costing. It keeps today's reliefs for employees under 21 and apprentices under 25 (paragraph 273).

- **The option the review sets aside.** It considered extending the under-21 relief to employees aged 21 to 24. Under that relief, employers pay no National Insurance on earnings up to £50,270. The review proposes "a different course" (paragraph 269). Citing the Resolution Foundation, it says the extension "would cost around £5.1 billion but only create approximately 38,000 additional jobs" (paragraph 268).

## Limitations

These results:

- hold employment fixed, where the review assumes a rise in employment that would add to the saving;
- leave out the Additional Cost Allowance, the Severe and Complex Needs Insurance and the Health and Disability Fund. This raises both the saving and the fall in incomes;
- remove the health element for every claimant under 25, where the review says "most". This raises both;
- end the entitlements that follow PIP, as today's rules do, which the review doesn't address. This raises both, by up to £[F4b] billion of the saving;
- keep the assessment status of claimants whose award no longer includes the health element, so they keep today's work allowance;
- use the UC housing element as the marker for living independently, as the review's annex does;
- set the work allowance at a level the review doesn't give;
- [give the health element's lower rate for claims from April 2026 to a random share of awards, as the data hold no claim dates;]
- [treat Scottish Adult Disability Payment as PIP / leave it unchanged;]
- end in 2031-32.

## Methodology

Our estimates come from PolicyEngine's [Milburn review dashboard](https://www.policyengine.org/uk/milburn-review). It runs the `policyengine` Python package (version 6.20.2) with PolicyEngine UK (version 2.177.7) on Microcosm UK build fcfac709, our survey-based dataset, certified in policyengine 6.20.2. [Prices and earnings: name the OBR forecast that PolicyEngine UK 2.177.7 uses.]

- **Static.** Each estimate holds behaviour fixed: people work, earn and claim as they would without the change.
- **Fully in force.** Except in the phased path, every eligible young person is on the new rules from April 2029.
- **Net income.** Deciles rank households by their income under current law, before housing costs, under the Households Below Average Income definition. The definition adjusts for household size, and each decile holds about a tenth of people.
- **Disclosure floor.** The dashboard withholds any result that rests on fewer than three survey households. [List any withheld cells.]

Every PolicyEngine figure in this briefing comes from the dashboard's published results ([commit or manifest]). Every figure from the review comes from its report, analytical annex or data tables, as cited.
