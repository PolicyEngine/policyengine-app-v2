In the Autumn Budget on 28 October 2026, the Chancellor announced that carers who earn more than the Carer's Allowance earnings limit will no longer lose the whole allowance. From a date within 2030-31, the allowance will instead fall by £1 for each £1 of weekly earnings above the limit, in England and Wales. This post explains the rules and shows their effect on two example carers in 2031-32, the first full year of the taper, using PolicyEngine UK household calculations. It ends with PolicyEngine's estimate of the cost.

Key figures for 2031-32:

- The Weekly Earnings Limit is 16 hours at the National Living Wage, rounded up to the next pound: £234 a week, on the OBR's forecast for April 2031.
- PolicyEngine UK projects Carer's Allowance at £98 a week.
- Under current rules, a carer whose earnings are above the limit by any amount loses the whole allowance. With the taper, the allowance falls by £1 for each £1 above the limit, so a carer with earnings for the limit of £333 a week or more gets none.
- Earnings for the limit are pay after income tax and National Insurance. For an employee with no pension contributions, the allowance runs out at gross pay of £366 a week.
- A carer who does not claim Universal Credit and earns £240 a week gains £75 a week. At £300 a week, the gain is £38.
- Universal Credit falls by £1 for each £1 of Carer's Allowance, and the allowance is taxable, so a carer on Universal Credit loses: about £8 a week at £240, once the tax on the allowance is deducted from earnings for Universal Credit, as we explain below.
- The Treasury estimates a cost of £40 million in 2030-31 and £80 million in 2031-32. We estimate a net cost of £27.5 million and £55.0 million.

## What the Budget announced

The [Budget 2026 document](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/budget-2026-document/budget-2026-html) (paragraph 6.13) and the Treasury's [policy costings](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ae1883fe4367e5c8b0467df/AB26_Policy_Costing.pdf) (page 16) describe the measure:

1. **The earnings limit.** Carer's Allowance is not paid for a week in which the carer's earnings are above the Weekly Earnings Limit. The costings set the limit at 16 times the National Living Wage. Since April 2025, the government has set the limit at 16 times the hourly National Living Wage rounded up to the nearest pound, as when it raised the limit from £151 to £196 a week in April 2025 ([Explanatory Memorandum to SI 2025/352](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/352/pdfs/uksiem_20250352_en_001.pdf), paragraphs 5.3 and 5.6).
2. **The taper.** Above the limit, the allowance is withdrawn at a rate of 100%: it falls by £1 for each £1 of earnings above the limit, instead of being lost in full.
3. **Earnings.** The test counts net earnings: gross pay less income tax, National Insurance and half of any pension contributions ([Social Security Benefit (Computation of Earnings) Regulations 1996](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1996/2745/regulation/10), regulation 10).
4. **Where.** The taper applies in England and Wales. In Scotland, Carer Support Payment has replaced Carer's Allowance, and Northern Ireland, where social security is devolved, is not included.
5. **When.** The taper begins within the 2030-31 financial year, on a date still to be confirmed.
6. **Who.** The government estimates that around 48,000 people benefit in 2030-31, including people who become newly entitled, of whom around 3,000 live in Wales ([Budget 2026 data sources](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ae1e9a2460c1346f7be77ff/Budget_2026_-_Data_Sources.pdf), page 30).

| -------- | ------------------------------------------: | --------------------: | ------------------------------------: | -----------------------------------------------: |
| 2030-31  |                                      £14.23 |                  £228 |                                   £96 |                                     £325 or more |
| 2031-32  |                                      £14.62 |                  £234 |                                   £98 |                                     £333 or more |

Carer's Allowance after 2026-27 is PolicyEngine UK's projection, uprated with inflation on the same forecast.

The Treasury estimates that the taper costs £40 million in 2030-31 and £80 million in 2031-32, and nothing in earlier years ([Budget 2026, Table 6.1](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ae187a2779378370fd80a9a/Table_6.1_and_6.2_Budget_2026_Policy_Decisions.xlsx), row 7). ## Worked examples

The examples follow two carers in the West Midlands in 2031-32. Each is 40, works as an employee with no pension contributions, and cares for 35 hours a week for a parent aged 72 who receives Attendance Allowance and lives in a separate household. Household net income is therefore the carer's own.

- **Carer 1** owns their home outright, has no housing costs and does not claim Universal Credit.
- **Carer 2** rents privately for £750 a month and claims Universal Credit. The award includes the carer element, £55 a week, and a housing element of £160 a week, below the rent because of the Local Housing Allowance.

Each table starts from gross pay and works out earnings for the limit. At or below the £234 limit, a carer gets the full allowance with or without the taper. Above it, the allowance is £0 without the taper and the full rate less the excess with it. Net income is gross pay less income tax and National Insurance, plus Carer's Allowance and Universal Credit, less the TV licence fee (£180 a year).

The examples assume that the current-law limit follows the government's rule, 16 × the National Living Wage rounded up to the next pound (£234 in 2031-32), not the £204 that PolicyEngine UK holds after 2026-27. So for carers earning between £204 and £234, the amounts with and without the taper differ from those on PolicyEngine's Autumn Budget dashboard, while the measure's cost and each household's change are the same either way.

Carer 1 gains most just above the limit, about £80 a week at £235, and the gain falls to zero at £366. Carer 2 loses throughout the taper range: by about £8 a week up to £247, and by less above that.

Universal Credit counts Carer's Allowance as unearned income and deducts it in full ([Universal Credit Regulations 2013](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/66), regulation 66(1)(b)(iii)). The carer element does not depend on Carer's Allowance. So for a carer on Universal Credit, each £1 of extra allowance takes £1 off Universal Credit. Carer's Allowance is also [taxable](https://www.gov.uk/income-tax/taxfree-and-taxable-state-benefits), and with the standard Personal Allowance every carer in the taper range pays income tax on it: below about £247 of pay, pay plus allowance is about £332 a week, above the Personal Allowance, and above £247 all of the allowance is taxed.

Universal Credit deducts from employed earnings the income tax paid in respect of the employment ([regulation 55(5)(b)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/55)). HMRC says that tax on taxable state benefits is [usually taken automatically from a person's other income](https://www.gov.uk/income-tax/how-you-pay-income-tax), and that a tax code can take account of taxable state benefits. For an employee whose tax code takes account of Carer's Allowance, the tax on the allowance is part of the PAYE taken from pay, and so lowers earnings for Universal Credit. Universal Credit's 55% taper gives back 55p of each £1 of that tax, so a carer on Universal Credit loses 45% of the tax on the extra allowance: about £8 a week at £240, £4 at £300 and £2 at £330.

This is our adjustment. PolicyEngine UK 2.151.0 does not yet count the PAYE on the allowance against Universal Credit earned income: it deducts only the tax on the pay itself, and so shows the whole of the tax on the allowance as a loss, about £17 a week at £240. The figures for this measure on PolicyEngine's Autumn Budget dashboard do not include the adjustment. We apply it to employees only. A self-employed carer has no pay for the tax to be taken from, so the adjustment leaves them as PolicyEngine UK has them, and this post does not cover them.

Even with the adjustment, carer 2 is better off without the allowance: without the taper, when pay rises from £234 to £235 and the allowance stops, net income rises by about £8.

Without the taper, carer 1 keeps 80p of each extra £1 of pay up to the limit: the allowance uses part of the Personal Allowance, so pay above about £148 a week is taxed at 20%. Above the limit, with no allowance, carer 1 keeps all of each extra £1 up to about £247 and 72p above it. From £247 to £366, carer 1 keeps 14.4p: income tax and National Insurance on the pay take 28p, the allowance falls by 72p, and the income tax on the allowance falls by 14.4p. This is an effective marginal tax rate of 85.6%, against 28% without the taper.

Carer 2 keeps 36p of each extra £1 up to the limit and 45p from £234 to about £247, with or without the taper. From £247 to £366, carer 2 keeps 38.9p with the taper, against 32.4p without, because the loss from the tax on the allowance shrinks as the allowance falls.

## Population estimates

We also ran the measure on PolicyEngine UK's microsimulation model, which uses survey data on UK households, projected to 2030-31 and 2031-32 on the OBR's October 2026 forecast. For consistency with the examples, we set the current-law limit to the government's rule on both sides (£228 in 2030-31 and £234 in 2031-32), and we apply the same Universal Credit adjustment for the PAYE on the allowance. The model applies half a year of the taper in 2030-31, as the start date is not yet set.

| £ million                               |  2030-31 |  2031-32 |
| --------------------------------------- | -------: | -------: |
| Higher Carer's Allowance                |     77.8 |    158.8 |
| Lower Universal Credit                  |    −35.0 |    −72.2 |
| Higher income tax                       |    −15.3 |    −31.6 |
| **Net cost**                            | **27.5** | **55.0** |
| Carer's Allowance less Universal Credit |     42.8 |     86.6 |

Lower Universal Credit and higher income tax offset part of the cost of the higher allowance. The current-law limit setting does not change the cost.

About 82,500 people have a higher Carer's Allowance in 2030-31, and about 82,900 in 2031-32, all in England. About half (51%) are in families on Universal Credit, for whom the higher allowance brings no gain. Our count of people with a higher allowance is larger than the Treasury's 48,000 people who benefit.

Our net cost, £27.5 million in 2030-31 and £55.0 million in 2031-32, is 69% of the Treasury's £40 million and £80 million. Carer's Allowance less Universal Credit, £42.8 million and £86.6 million, is 7% to 8% above the Treasury's figures. The Treasury's figures lie between the two; we cannot tell which items its costing includes. Its costing does include a behavioural response, in which some carers increase their earnings or enter work, that lowers the cost. Our model has no such response.

These estimates have important limits:

- The survey sample has no carers in Wales whom the taper affects, so we estimate none there, against the Treasury's 3,000.
- The estimate rests on fewer than 50 survey households, so it is imprecise, and we do not show a breakdown by income decile.

## Assumptions and limitations

- The worked examples are household calculations for single carers. The carer and the parent live in separate households, so the parent's income does not count.
- Weekly amounts are annual amounts divided by 52, so pay is the same each week. The earnings test is weekly, so a carer whose pay varies can be above the limit in some weeks and below it in others.
- PolicyEngine UK works out earnings for the limit as pay less National Insurance and the income tax that PAYE would take on the pay alone with a standard tax code. It does not model the deduction for the cost of care while the carer is at work, which can be up to half of net earnings, so it can overstate earnings for carers who pay for care.
- PolicyEngine UK holds the current-law earnings limit at £204 after 2026-27. In the worked examples and the population estimates, we set it to the government's rule with and without the taper: £228 in 2030-31 and £234 in 2031-32, the same limit the taper uses. This does not change the measure's cost or any household's change, because the taper changes nothing for carers at or below the limit.
- The worked examples and the population estimates both count the PAYE on Carer's Allowance against Universal Credit earned income for employees, which PolicyEngine UK 2.151.0 and the dashboard do not yet do. Self-employed carers are outside the scope of this adjustment and of this post.
- Carer's Allowance, Universal Credit and the National Living Wage for 2030-31 and 2031-32 are projections. PolicyEngine UK does not round its projected Carer's Allowance to the penny.
- The examples assume that each carer claims Carer's Allowance and, for carer 2, Universal Credit. The population estimates use the take-up assumptions in PolicyEngine's data.
- Scotland's Carer Support Payment is not affected. In Northern Ireland, Carer's Allowance keeps the current rule.

## Methodology

We used the `policyengine` Python package (version 6.11.1) with PolicyEngine UK (version 2.151.0). PolicyEngine UK does not yet include the taper, so we applied the `carers_allowance_earnings_taper` modifier from the draft update to PolicyEngine's Autumn Budget 2026 dashboard: branch `carers-taper-limit-rule`, built on pull request #290 of `PolicyEngine/uk-autumn-budget-dashboard-2026` at `ad32af7f`, at commit `004c5281`. The next commit, `38163ede`, changes only the modifier's documentation. The branch differs from #290 in the limit: #290 moves the April 2027 limit (16 hours at that year's National Living Wage) on with average earnings and does not round it, while the branch uses the OBR's National Living Wage forecast for each April and rounds up to the next pound, which gives £228 in 2030-31 and £234 in 2031-32.

Both sides of every calculation use the OBR's October 2026 forecast, as the dashboard does, with PolicyEngine UK's current-law earnings limit set to the government's rule. Both sides also use our Universal Credit adjustment: for a person with employment income, the income tax that Universal Credit deducts from earnings is worked out with Carer's Allowance counted as part of the earnings, so it includes the tax on the allowance. We calculated each household for gross pay from £150 to £400 a week in £1 steps. The charts plot whole-pound points where the schedules change slope and a point every £20. PolicyEngine UK reduces all employees' pay by 0.16% in 2031-32, to reflect employers passing on the cost of the salary sacrifice cap, so we set pay 0.16% higher to give whole-pound gross pay.
