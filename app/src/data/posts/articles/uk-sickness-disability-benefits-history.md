On 13 January 1913, the first people insured under the [National Insurance Act 1911](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Geo5/1-2/55/pdfs/ukpga_19110055_en.pdf) became [entitled to sickness benefit](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1913/jan/07/sick-and-maternity-benefit): 10 shillings a week for men and 7 shillings and 6 pence for women. On the definition we set out below, Great Britain has had 16 national benefits for people who are sick or disabled since then. Six are open to new claims in 2026: Employment and Support Allowance (ESA), Industrial Injuries Disablement Benefit, Attendance Allowance, Disability Living Allowance (DLA, now for children only), the Personal Independence Payment (PIP) and Statutory Sick Pay. Universal Credit (UC) also pays a health element to claimants found to have limited capability for work and work-related activity. This post sets out when each benefit started and ended, and how spending, caseloads and rates have changed.

- **Benefits open to new claims:** one in 1913, seven from 1976 to 1991, eight from 1992 to 1994, and six in 2026.
- **Spending:** the benefits in Figures 2 and 3 cost £77.9 billion in 2025-26, 2.54% of GDP (Great Britain, with England and Wales only for five benefits from 2020-21, and with UC included from 2019-20): 1.47% on the extra-costs benefits, 1.04% on out-of-work sickness benefits including UC for people with health conditions, and 0.02% on industrial injuries benefits.
- **Largest caseloads in 2025-26:** 3.88 million people received PIP in England and Wales, and DWP counted 3.14 million UC awards in which someone had limited capability for work, with or without work-related activity, or was waiting for an assessment.
- **2026 changes:** under the Universal Credit Act 2025, new UC claims from 6 April 2026 receive a health element of £217.26 a month, against £429.80 for claims that already had it and for people who meet new severe conditions criteria or are terminally ill. The Act also sets the standard allowance at least 2.3% above its inflation-only level in 2026-27.
- **Reviews in November 2026:** the Milburn review of young people not in education, employment or training (NEET) proposes replacing the UC health element and PIP for people under 25 with a Youth Allowance, an Additional Cost Allowance and other support, without setting their amounts. The Timms review sets out changes to PIP.

## The benefits, 1911 to 2026

Table 1 lists every benefit we count, with the date it opened to new claims and the date it closed to them. We count national benefits paid by the state, or by employers under a statutory duty, where entitlement rests on sickness, incapacity for work or disability. We count a benefit that was renamed or extended to a new group once, and leave out elements and premiums inside other benefits, such as UC's health element and the disability premiums in Income Support; carers' benefits; survivors' benefits; one-off lump sums; military schemes; and benefits paid only in Scotland.

## Before the welfare state, 1897 to 1948

The [Workmen's Compensation Act 1897](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/60-61/37/pdfs/ukpga_18970037_en.pdf) made employers liable to compensate workmen injured at work, from 1 July 1898; the state paid nothing. The 1911 Act created National Health Insurance for employed workers. Contributions began on [15 July 1912](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1912/mar/11/operation-of-act), and sickness benefit was payable after 26 weeks of insurance and 26 weekly contributions. Under the Act's Fourth Schedule, sickness benefit lasted up to 26 weeks; after that, disablement benefit paid 5 shillings a week to people insured for at least 104 weeks.

## National Insurance, 1948

The [National Insurance Act 1946](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Geo6/9-10/67/pdfs/ukpga_19460067_en.pdf) replaced health insurance with a single National Insurance scheme, which started on [5 July 1948](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1947/jun/09/national-insurance-and-health-schemes). Sir William Beveridge's 1942 report, _Social Insurance and Allied Services_, which the House of Commons [debated in February 1943](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1943/feb/16/social-insurance-and-allied-services), came before it. The new sickness benefit paid 26 shillings a week to a single adult, with additions for dependants. On the same day the [National Insurance (Industrial Injuries) Act 1946](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Geo6/9-10/62/pdfs/ukpga_19460062_en.pdf) replaced workmen's compensation with state injury benefit, at 45 shillings a week, and a disablement pension scaled to the degree of disablement.

## Long-term sickness and the extra costs of disability, 1971 to 1977

In 1971 two new benefits opened. [Invalidity Benefit](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1972/jan/25/invalidity-allowance-and-pension), from 23 September 1971, replaced sickness benefit for people who had been incapable of work for 168 days and met its contribution conditions ([National Insurance Act 1971, s. 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1971/50/pdfs/ukpga_19710050_en.pdf)). [Attendance Allowance](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1971/jan/13/disabled-persons-attendance-allowance), from 6 December 1971, paid [£4.80 a week](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1971/jun/17/social-security-supplementary-benefits) to people who needed care because of severe disability.

The Non-contributory Invalidity Pension, for people unable to work who had not paid enough contributions, followed on [20 November 1975](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1975/jul/08/non-contributory-invalidity-pension), and a version for married women in [November 1977](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1977/apr/29/disabled-housewives). Mobility Allowance, at [£5 a week](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1975/60/pdfs/ukpga_19750060_en.pdf), opened in stages by age from [1 January 1976](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1977/may/17/mobility-allowance).

## Employers and severe disablement, 1983 to 1986

From 6 April 1983, the [Social Security and Housing Benefits Act 1982](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1982/24/enacted) required employers to pay Statutory Sick Pay for up to eight weeks' worth in any period of sickness or tax year, and abolished injury benefit ([commencement order](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1982/893/made)). The Act set three rates from £25 to £37 a week depending on earnings; the Statutory Sick Pay Up-rating Order 1982 raised them to £27.20, £33.75 and £40.25 before the scheme began ([House of Lords debate](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/lords/1983/jan/25/statutory-sick-pay-up-rating-order-1982)). The [Social Security Act 1985](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1985/53/pdfs/ukpga_19850053_en.pdf) extended Statutory Sick Pay to 28 weeks from April 1986. Severe Disablement Allowance replaced both non-contributory invalidity pensions on [29 November 1984](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1992/dec/03/invalidity-pension).

## DLA and Incapacity Benefit, 1992 to 2001

On 6 April 1992, under the [Disability Living Allowance and Disability Working Allowance Act 1991](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/21/enacted), DLA replaced Mobility Allowance, and replaced Attendance Allowance for people claiming before 65 ([commencement order](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1991/2617/made)). Disability Working Allowance, an in-work payment for disabled workers, opened the same day; the [Tax Credits Act 1999](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1999/10/enacted) renamed it Disabled Person's Tax Credit from [October 1999](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1999/10/notes/division/2), and the [Tax Credits Act 2002](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2002/21/section/11) folded it into the disability element of Working Tax Credit in April 2003.

On 13 April 1995, Incapacity Benefit replaced sickness benefit and Invalidity Benefit ([commencement order](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1994/2926/made)). The [Social Security (Incapacity for Work) Act 1994](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1994/18/enacted) introduced the "all work test", which assessed how far a claimant's condition limited their ability to perform prescribed activities. Severe Disablement Allowance closed to new claims on 6 April 2001 ([Welfare Reform and Pensions Act 1999, s. 65](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1999/30/section/65)).

## ESA, Universal Credit and PIP, 2008 to 2013

ESA replaced Incapacity Benefit for new claims on 27 October 2008, under the [Welfare Reform Act 2007](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2007/5/contents) ([commencement order](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2008/787/made)). Its Work Capability Assessment placed claimants found to have limited capability for work in a work-related activity group, and those also found to have limited capability for work-related activity in a support group, each with its own addition to the basic allowance. DWP reassessed existing Incapacity Benefit and Severe Disablement Allowance claimants for ESA from 2011, and most had moved [by 2014](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/esa-underpayments-progress-on-checking-june-2021/june-2021-esa-underpayments-numbers-affected-expenditure-and-final-update-on-checking). From 1 May 2012, the [Welfare Reform Act 2012](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2012/5/section/51) limited contributory ESA outside the support group to 365 days ([commencement order](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/863/made)).

UC began on [29 April 2013](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/983/made). It applies the same assessment and adds a limited capability for work element (£123.62 a month in 2013) or a limited capability for work and work-related activity element (£303.66) to the standard allowance ([UC Regulations 2013](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/made)). PIP replaced DLA for new claims from people aged 16 to State Pension age: in some postcodes from [8 April 2013](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/358/made), and everywhere from [10 June 2013](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/1250/made). Children under 16 [still claim DLA](https://www.gov.uk/dla-disability-living-allowance-benefit) in England and Wales.

## Changes since 2017

From 3 April 2017, the [Welfare Reform and Work Act 2016](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2016/7/section/15) removed the ESA work-related activity component and, through [section 16](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2016/7/section/16), the UC limited capability for work element for new claims ([commencement](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/111/made); [savings for existing claims](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/204/made)).

From 6 April 2026, the [Employment Rights Act 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/36/section/10) made Statutory Sick Pay payable from the first day of sickness absence and, under [section 11](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/36/section/11), extended it to employees earning below the lower earnings limit, at the lower of a flat rate and 80% of earnings. In 2026-27 the flat rate is [£123.25 a week](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/millions-of-workers-get-new-access-to-sick-pay-and-parental-leave).

The [Universal Credit Act 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/22/enacted) received Royal Assent on 3 September 2025. It:

- sets the UC health element for claims made from 6 April 2026 at £217.26 a month ([section 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/22/section/2)), except for people who already had the element and have kept it since, people who meet new severe conditions criteria, and people who are terminally ill;
- removes the annual uprating duty for the health element and for the limited capability for work element, which claims from before April 2017 keep, for the tax years 2026-27 to 2029-30 ([section 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/22/section/3));
- requires the protected health element and the standard allowance together to rise at least with inflation each year to 2029-30 ([section 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/22/section/4));
- requires the standard allowance to be at least 2.3% above its inflation-only level in 2026-27, rising to 4.8% in 2029-30 ([section 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/22/section/1)).

In 2026-27, DWP's [rates table](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/benefit-and-pension-rates-2026-to-2027/proposed-benefit-and-pension-rates-2026-to-2027) gives the protected health element as £429.80 a month and the standard allowance for a single claimant aged 25 or over as £424.90, up from £400.14 in 2025-26.

Spending on these benefits was 0.54% of GDP in 1949-50, 1.08% in 1978-79, 1.79% in 1995-96, 1.48% in 2008-09 and 2.54% in 2025-26. These totals do not cover the same benefits throughout. The 1949-50 figure is sickness benefit alone: the extra-costs benefits did not yet exist, and industrial injuries benefits are not shown before 1970-71. Means-tested support for sick and disabled people counts through income-related ESA from 2008-09 and UC from 2019-20, but not through Income Support or its predecessors, and UC is missing for 2014-15 to 2018-19. Scottish spending on several benefits drops out from 2020-21. Spending on the extra-costs benefits has exceeded spending on the out-of-work sickness benefits, including UC for people with health conditions, in every year since 1999-00; in 2025-26 the two were 1.47% and 1.04% of GDP. Statutory Sick Pay appears in DWP's tables while DWP reimbursed employers for it; employers have paid it in full [since April 2014](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/benefit-expenditure-and-caseload-tables-2026) (note 14 of the [tables](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6b00617707e86cba56db9779/Benefit-Expenditure-and-Caseloads-Tables-Autumn-2026..xlsx)), and the tables show DWP-funded amounts of £50 million in 2020-21 and £66 million in 2021-22.

From 2020-21 the tables leave out Scottish spending on Attendance Allowance, DLA, PIP, Severe Disablement Allowance and Industrial Injuries Disablement Benefit, after the Scottish Government took over these benefits in Scotland. The shares of GDP compare spending in Great Britain, or England and Wales for these benefits from 2020-21, with UK GDP.

## The main rates over time

Table 2 gives the weekly rate for a single adult without dependants at the introduction of each main out-of-work sickness benefit, and in 2026-27, in cash and at April 2025 prices. For Incapacity Benefit it gives the long-term rate without age additions, and for ESA the support group rate without premiums: the main rate of each for a single adult. For 2026-27 it gives the new style ESA rate; income-related ESA pays £97.75 plus a £48.50 support component. Real values are at April 2025 prices, the base year of DWP's Abstract of benefit rate statistics, so the 2026-27 real values are below the cash rates. The Retail Prices Index (RPI) is the only price index that covers the whole period; the Consumer Prices Index (CPI) starts in 1988. RPI has risen faster than CPI since 1988, so rates from before 2025 are lower in April 2025 prices on CPI than on RPI. From the Incapacity Benefit long-term rate in 1995 to the ESA support group rate in 2026-27, the real change is −11% on RPI and +17% on CPI. ## The Timms and Milburn reviews

On 24 November 2026, DWP published the [final report of the Timms review of PIP](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-timms-review-final-report/final-report-of-the-timms-review-of-personal-independence-payment), co-chaired by Sir Stephen Timms, Sharon Brennan and Dr Clenton Farquharson. The report states that its recommendations do not constitute government policy. It makes 15 recommendations for PIP as it stands, among them fewer award reviews for lifelong or degenerative conditions, a simpler application form, independent advocacy and more capacity for face-to-face assessments. It also describes a reformed benefit with an initial gateway, fast-track routes for serious, well-evidenced conditions and for people at the end of life, and an assessment of the impact of a condition that includes an element for extra costs.

On 17 November 2026, DWP published [_From a Welfare State to a Working State_](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/from-a-welfare-state-to-a-working-state-a-new-settlement-for-the-next-generation), Alan Milburn's independent review of the rise in the number of young people who are NEET. For people under 25, the review proposes:

- a single Youth Allowance, paid at the under-25 UC standard allowance with a higher rate for young people living independently, which involves abolishing the UC health element and removing the need for the Work Capability Assessment;
- a local Health and Disability Fund for extra costs during support into work or education, serious illness or treatment, or while longer-term needs are unclear;
- an Additional Cost Allowance to replace PIP, for young people whose disability-related extra costs are significant, established and ongoing;
- Severe and Complex Needs Insurance, a long-term award without a qualifying period for young people with the most profound, long-term disabilities, who the review says will likely never be able to work.

The review proposes a phased rollout for new claimants, subject to legislation, and would move existing claimants at their scheduled reassessment. PolicyEngine's [Milburn review dashboard](https://www.policyengine.org/uk/milburn-review) and [briefing](https://www.policyengine.org/uk/research/uk-milburn-review-briefing) model some of the review's proposals.

## Data and methods

The [data and code behind this post](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/uk-sickness-benefits-history) include every source file's address, the scripts that build the figures from DWP's and the OBR's spreadsheets, the source and status of each date in Table 1, and the household calculation.

- **Timeline.** Each date in Table 1 comes from the Act, statutory instrument, Hansard record or DWP page linked beside it. National Health Insurance ended when the National Insurance scheme began on 5 July 1948.
- **Spending and caseloads.** DWP's [benefit expenditure and caseload tables, Autumn Budget 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6b00617707e86cba56db9779/Benefit-Expenditure-and-Caseloads-Tables-Autumn-2026..xlsx), Table 1a for spending and the benefit tables for caseloads, cover outturn to 2025-26. Sickness benefit in 1948-49 covers the part of the year from 5 July 1948. Severe Disablement Allowance includes the non-contributory invalidity pensions that preceded it. Industrial injuries spending is the annually managed part, without the pneumoconiosis and mesothelioma lump-sum schemes. Incapacity Benefit and ESA caseloads include people who receive National Insurance credits only; Attendance Allowance, DLA and PIP caseloads count everyone entitled, including people entitled but not currently paid.
- **GDP.** We divide spending by UK nominal GDP for each financial year from the OBR's [public finances databank, October 2026](https://obr.uk/download/public-finances-databank-october-2026-efo/) (ONS series BKTL).
- **Household example.** [PolicyEngine UK](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/policyengine-uk/tree/2.179.2) version 2.179.2, which applies the Universal Credit Act 2025.
