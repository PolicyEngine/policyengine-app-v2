On 24 November 2026 the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) [published](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-timms-review-final-report) the final report of the Timms Review of [Personal Independence Payment](https://www.gov.uk/pip) (PIP), _Making PIP fit for purpose: A new vision_. Its steering group, co-chaired by Sharon Brennan, Dr Clenton Farquharson and Sir Stephen Timms, makes 15 recommendations to improve the current benefit and sets out principles for a reformed one. The report states that its recommendations "do not constitute government policy" ([p. 6](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6b0453255d1e916c0914591b/print-ready-main-report.pdf#page=10)). It proposes no change to PIP's rates, components, points thresholds or age limits, and its terms of reference require PIP spending to stay within the [Office for Budget Responsibility's](https://obr.uk/) (OBR) forecast ([p. 15](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6b0453255d1e916c0914591b/print-ready-main-report.pdf#page=19)).

The change with the clearest effect on incomes is one the report discusses rather than recommends: the government's plan, set out in the [Pathways to Work Green Paper](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/pathways-to-work-reforming-benefits-and-support-to-get-britain-working-green-paper), to make the PIP daily living component the single route to the [Universal Credit (UC) health element](https://www.gov.uk/health-conditions-disability-universal-credit). The report says "not all of the steering group endorse this commitment" ([p. 119](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6b0453255d1e916c0914591b/print-ready-main-report.pdf#page=123)). The government has not said how existing awards would be treated, so we model three versions for 2029-30 with [PolicyEngine UK](https://www.policyengine.org/uk/model)'s static microsimulation.

Key findings:

- **Every award:** UC spending falls by £3.2 billion; 703,000 claimants lose the health element and 188,000 gain it and are better off.
- **New claims only:** UC spending rises by £0.1 billion; 134,000 lose the element and 188,000 gain it and are better off.
- **Existing awards protected:** UC spending rises by £0.5 billion; no one loses the element and 188,000 gain it and are better off.
- In every version, about 39,000 awards gain the element for a claimant who cares for a severely disabled person and lose the carer element, which is worth more, so their UC falls by about £135 a year.
- Applied to every award, absolute poverty before housing costs rises by 148,000 people (0.2 percentage points). Most claimants who would lose the element receive no PIP at all (636,000 of 703,000), and the average family losing it would see UC fall by about £5,200 a year.

Most of the report's other recommendations cannot be estimated yet; the methodology explains why.

## What the report recommends

Most of the [report's](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-timms-review-final-report) 15 recommendations concern how DWP runs PIP rather than who receives it or how much: fewer [award reviews](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-timms-review-final-report) for lifelong conditions, more support during [hospital stays](https://www.gov.uk/pip/hospital-hospice-or-care-home), better handling of fluctuating conditions, and changes to forms, assessments and digital services. For the longer term it sets out principles for a reformed benefit, with fast-track awards, an assessment of extra costs and a flexible mix of payments, but no rates, eligibility criteria or costs ([pp. 110–116](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6b0453255d1e916c0914591b/print-ready-main-report.pdf#page=114)). The reformed benefit would remain non-means-tested, paid in cash and not conditional on work (pp. [10](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6b0453255d1e916c0914591b/print-ready-main-report.pdf#page=14) and [110](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6b0453255d1e916c0914591b/print-ready-main-report.pdf#page=114)).

## PIP daily living as the route to the UC health element

The [Pathways to Work Green Paper](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/pathways-to-work-reforming-benefits-and-support-to-get-britain-working-green-paper) plans to abolish the Work Capability Assessment and let entitlement to PIP daily living give access to the UC health element. The report treats this as a commitment the government made before the review. It recognises that a single route could spare people two assessments, says that not all of the steering group endorse it, asks for an impact assessment of who may no longer be eligible for health-related support, and says it should proceed only if it meets the group's six tests ([pp. 119–120](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6b0453255d1e916c0914591b/print-ready-main-report.pdf#page=123)). The government has not published the rules, so we model three versions in 2029-30:

- **Every award.** A UC award includes the health element only if a claimant receives PIP daily living. Awards that keep it keep their current rate.
- **New claims only.** Awards at the rate for claims made from April 2026 need a claimant on PIP daily living; awards at the earlier, protected rate keep the element. This is wider than applying the route only to claims made after the Work Capability Assessment ends, which the Green Paper's [indicative timetable](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/pathways-to-work-reforming-benefits-and-support-to-get-britain-working-green-paper/pathways-to-work-reforming-benefits-and-support-to-get-britain-working-green-paper#annex-a-summary-of-policy-measures-and-consultation-questions) puts in 2028/29. The data have no claim dates, so the model gives the new-claim rate to the most recent claims by the survey's UC start date, until they make up the expected share of awards (22% in 2029-30).
- **Existing awards protected.** Every award with the health element keeps it.

In all three, awards with a claimant on PIP daily living but no element gain it at the rate for new claims, and each claimant's assessment of limited capability for work is unchanged, so the UC work allowance that goes with it still applies. An award without the element also loses the [benefit cap](https://www.gov.uk/benefit-cap) exemption that rests on it. Under [regulation 29(4)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/29) of the Universal Credit Regulations 2013, a claimant for whom the health element is included can't also have the [carer element](https://www.gov.uk/universal-credit/what-youll-get) for caring for a severely disabled person. So a claimant who loses the health element and is a carer receives the carer element instead, and a claimant who gains it and is a carer loses the carer element. At £228.51 a month in 2029-30, the carer element is worth more than the health element at the new-claim rate. The health element is £217.26 a month for claims from April 2026 under the [Universal Credit Act 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/22/contents) and, in PolicyEngine UK's projection, £460.80 a month for earlier claims. The data can't identify people who meet the severe conditions criteria ([p. 120](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6b0453255d1e916c0914591b/print-ready-main-report.pdf#page=124)), so in the first two versions they are treated like anyone else without PIP daily living.

### How far PIP and the UC health element overlap

The results depend on how far the two benefits overlap. Under current law in 2029-30, 2.88 million people receive the UC health element as claimants with limited capability for work and work-related activity; 2.15 million of them (75%) also receive PIP daily living. In [DWP's statistics](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/universal-credit-health-statistics-april-2019-to-june-2026/universal-credit-health-statistics-april-2019-to-june-2026), 2.8 million people in Great Britain had limited capability for work-related activity in June 2026, close to our 2.75 million for Great Britain in 2026-27. [DWP's evidence pack for the review](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/timms-review-of-personal-independence-payment-interim-report/annex-b-dwp-evidence-pack) finds that, in England and Wales in May 2024, 72% of people with limited capability for work-related activity in UC, or its equivalent in Employment and Support Allowance, received some PIP; our figure for England and Wales in 2026-27 is 77%. If the true overlap is lower than ours, more people would lose the element without protection and the fall in UC spending would be larger.

The gap is wider for young people. We estimate that 208,000 people aged 16 to 24 receive the element and 171,000 (82%) also receive PIP daily living. The Resolution Foundation [reports](https://www.resolutionfoundation.org/press-releases/government-must-match-milburns-ambition-to-properly-invest-in-young-people/) that 110,000 of 176,000 (62.5%) also receive PIP, without naming the component; its release does not state the data source, year or area, so the figures may not be directly comparable with ours.

## PIP under current law

The report's terms of reference keep PIP spending within the OBR forecast. [DWP's benefit expenditure and caseload tables](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/benefit-expenditure-and-caseload-tables-2026), consistent with the OBR's October 2026 forecast, project £41.2 billion of PIP spending and 4.9 million recipients in England and Wales in 2029-30. Under current law in 2029-30, PolicyEngine UK estimates:

- 4.3 million people receive PIP, or [Adult Disability Payment](https://www.mygov.scot/adult-disability-payment) in Scotland, at a cost of £34.9 billion, of which £22.8 billion is for daily living and the rest for mobility.
- 2.2 million receive the enhanced daily living rate and 1.9 million the standard rate.
- 653,000 of the 3.6 million recipients of working age are in work, or 18%, consistent with the report's "fewer than 20%" ([p. 56](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6b0453255d1e916c0914591b/print-ready-main-report.pdf#page=60)).
- 2.6 million recipients live in families receiving UC.

Our figures are below DWP's: we estimate 3.8 million recipients in England and Wales in 2029-30, against DWP's forecast of 4.9 million, and our UK spending, including Scotland, is below DWP's forecast for England and Wales alone. Our England and Wales caseload is also below the 4.1 million in DWP's [statistics](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/personal-independence-payment-statistics-to-july-2026/personal-independence-payment-statistics-to-july-2026) for July 2026.

## How it relates to the Milburn review

A week earlier, [Alan Milburn's review of young people and work](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/from-a-welfare-state-to-a-working-state-a-new-settlement-for-the-next-generation) proposed ending PIP and the UC health element for people under 25 and replacing PIP with three new forms of support. We estimate that ending the two without the replacements would save £4.4 billion in 2029-30 (see our [Milburn briefing](https://www.policyengine.org/uk/research/uk-milburn-review-briefing) and [dashboard](https://www.policyengine.org/uk/milburn-review)). The Timms Review makes no recommendation by age and names the Milburn review twice: among the reviews running alongside it ([p. 20](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6b0453255d1e916c0914591b/print-ready-main-report.pdf#page=24)), and in saying that joining up the reviews' findings "does not suggest endorsement" ([p. 124](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6b0453255d1e916c0914591b/print-ready-main-report.pdf#page=128)). Its principles do not differ by age. The government has not yet responded to either review.

## Methodology

We modelled the route to the UC health element by rewriting each benefit unit's health element in 2029-30 after [PolicyEngine UK](https://www.policyengine.org/uk/model) applies the UC rebalancing rules from the [Universal Credit Act 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/22/contents), then recalculating UC, household net income and poverty. A claimant passes the route when they are a UC claimant receiving PIP daily living above zero. Counts of claimants losing the element cover awards whose UC falls, and counts of claimants gaining it cover awards whose UC rises; awards gaining the element whose UC falls, because the carer element goes, are counted separately. For an award that gains the element, we take it to be included for a claimant on PIP daily living who isn't a carer if there is one, and otherwise the elder. Results cover the UK; for Scotland, the data record Adult Disability Payment as PIP. Every published figure rests on at least three survey households. The code and full results are in our [GitHub repository](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/uk-pip-review-2026/tree/main/analysis/timms-review). Page numbers are the report's printed page numbers.

### What we can't estimate

Most of the report's recommendations can't be estimated from the [survey data](https://www.policyengine.org/uk/model/data) PolicyEngine UK uses. The data record whether someone receives PIP, but not review dates, scores for each activity or long hospital stays, so the changes to award reviews, fluctuating conditions and the hospital rule are out of reach. The reformed benefit's fast-track pathways, impact assessment, flexible payments and rebalancing of eligibility have no criteria or amounts yet. When the government responds, we will estimate any changes it sets out that the model can express.
